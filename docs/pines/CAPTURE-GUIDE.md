# The Pines — Field Capture Guide (Mon–Fri on-site)

Goal for the week: a photograph of **every page of every document**, a filled-in
capture log, and verified backups. Nothing else. OCR, cleanup, and organizing
happen later, from home.

## The rig (30 minutes, Monday)

- **Camera:** your phone. Legal-size paper doesn't fit consumer flatbeds, and
  bound diaries can't be fed through a scanner; a phone handles both.
- **Stand:** anything that holds the phone steady, level, and pointed straight
  down: a tripod with a horizontal arm, or an improvised copy stand (a board
  across two stacks of books, phone lens over the edge). Steady + repeatable
  framing is what buys speed.
- **Surface:** a dark, matte background (dark towel or poster board) so page
  edges auto-detect cleanly.
- **Light:** indirect daylight near a window, or two lamps at ~45° from either
  side. No flash, no overhead glare, no shadows of your hands.
- **App:** any batch document scanner with auto edge-detection and auto-capture
  — Apple Notes/Files scanner, Google Drive scan, Microsoft Lens, Genius Scan.
  Pick whichever exports **full-resolution images** (not just compressed PDF)
  and test the export on Monday before trusting it with 1,000 pages. Plain
  camera app in a fixed position is a fine fallback.
- **Settings:** highest resolution available; lock focus/exposure once per
  session if the app allows.

## Naming and logging

One ID scheme, decided Monday, never changed:

```
PINES_B01_F03_D012_P01.jpg
       │    │    │    └─ page
       │    │    └────── document within folder
       │    └─────────── folder within box
       └──────────────── box (or shelf/binder — whatever the physical unit is)
```

Mirror the archive's existing physical organization — don't reorganize, don't
re-sort, and return everything exactly where it was. If the collection already
has labels or a finding aid, photograph those too; they're metadata.

Log each **document** (not each page) in `capture-log.template.csv` as you shoot
it. Ten seconds per row now saves hours of "what was this?" later. If typing on
a phone is slow, say the row aloud into a voice memo and transcribe rows at
night.

## Per-document procedure

1. Assign the next document ID; say/log the row.
2. Shoot every page in order, including blank backs if anything shows through,
   covers, envelopes, and attached notes.
3. Oversized items (schematics, maps): one overview shot, then overlapping
   section shots at readable distance.
4. Fragile bound diaries: open flat only as far as the binding safely allows;
   shoot page pairs; do not press the spine.
5. Anything with condition problems (tears, mold, fading) — note it in the log;
   flag "recapture" if a shot looked bad.

## Daily rhythm

| Day | Focus |
|-----|-------|
| **Mon** | Build rig, choose app, define IDs, set up cloud folder. **Pilot: capture 10–20 documents end-to-end**, export, back up, and zoom in on the results — confirm handwriting is legible at 100% before bulk capture. |
| **Tue–Thu** | Bulk capture, ~3 × 90-minute sessions/day in priority order (see PLAN.md: handwritten first, then company records, then legal, then schematics, then printed). Between sessions: record cousin interviews. |
| **Fri** | Gap sweep: recapture flagged shots, grab missed folders, photograph box/folder labels and the room itself, final backup verification, restore the physical archive exactly as found. |

**Nightly, non-negotiable (20 min):**

1. Export the day's images off the phone to the laptop.
2. Sync laptop copy to cloud storage (private folder).
3. Spot-check ~5% of the day's pages at full zoom; add bad ones to Friday's
   recapture list.
4. Confirm the capture log row count roughly matches the day's document count.

Three copies by bedtime: phone, laptop, cloud.

## The other archive: the cousins

The people who know what these documents *are* — who archived them, whose
handwriting is whose, what Skaneateles China was, which brother built what —
are on the property this week and won't all be reachable later. This context
exists nowhere on paper.

- Record voice memos: "walk me through this box," "who is this in this photo,"
  "tell me about the company."
- Get names/dates for the founding generation (three brothers, three sisters)
  down explicitly — it seeds the Phase 2 entity registry and family graph.
- Note who to follow up with about which topics, for the review phase.

## What NOT to do this week

- Don't OCR, crop, deskew, rename retroactively, or reorganize files.
- Don't change the ID scheme mid-week, even if a better one occurs to you.
- Don't skip the nightly backup because the day ran long — it's the only step
  that protects against losing the week.
- Don't leave Friday without photographing the labels, the boxes, and the
  storage location itself.
